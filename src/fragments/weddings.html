Unscripted by design

Most of your day will happen without direction, so we photograph it that way. Close when it counts. Out of sight when the moment is yours. The portraits are the only part that's staged, and even those keep moving so they feel like something you lived rather than something you posed for.

The day

The day starts before it has decided what it will be. We move through it quietly, close when it counts and unseen when the room belongs to you. Plans shift on a wedding day; keeping that shift invisible is most of the work.

The small things

Everything chosen on purpose gets kept on purpose: the rings, the letters, the borrowed, the red.

Portraits

Your wedding isn't spent posing. We direct in motion, never stiff, so most couples loosen within the first fifteen minutes. Those who'd rather stay close to their guests need only twenty minutes during cocktail hour.

The people

Some frames are planned. The best ones happen while everyone forgets the camera is in the room.

The party

Later, once the vows are kept and the formalities have burned away, the night loosens into something no timeline can hold. It is the least composed hour and the most worth keeping, the room at full volume, everyone finally and entirely themselves.

The unexpected

Not every frame is planned. Sometimes a horse at the fence line becomes the one everyone remembers.

The toasts

Somebody always cries. It is never who they predicted.

The practical

The unglamorous things, promised plainly. Your full gallery is in your hands within three to four weeks, depending on the season. A second photographer when the day runs in two directions at once. Three cameras, doubled cards, every frame backed up before the night is over, so nothing you lived is ever left to chance.

Afterward

"We had a blast." "Wonderful from start to finish." "We can't stop looking at the photos."

The line that keeps returning in the notes couples send: it felt easy. Comfort has a way of showing up in the pictures. The rest is craft, and answering quickly.

The quiet after

When the room finally empties, what stays is how it felt.

On first looks

A first look buys a calmer timeline and a cocktail hour spent with your guests. Skipping it keeps the reveal at the aisle. Both are right answers, and an easy one to settle together on the consultation call.

On planners

Book at least a month-of coordinator. The day itself is too late to learn a wedding, and without one the vendors end up planning it live. Coordination with yours is handled directly.

On phones

An unplugged ceremony protects the photographs. Guests stay in the moment, the aisle stays clear, and a friend with a Polaroid at the first dance is always welcome.

On light

Dark venue, hard flash, a tent lit only by candles. Good. Some couples book for exactly that, and where the light is not, it can be built.
